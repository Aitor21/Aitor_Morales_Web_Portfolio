What I built

I co-founded the studio and led design on the game. That meant the core verb — reading a profile and committing to a swipe — and the systems that give it weight: the daily match limit, the thirst meter that keeps rising while you deliberate, and the tension bar that runs a date.

I built what I designed, in Unity, and worked alongside my co-founders across art, narrative and engineering rather than handing specs over a wall.

02 — The collision

Two games, one swipe

Design DNA

Papers, Please × Reigns = Love at First Bite

Take the suspicious reading of documents from Papers, Please, the swipe-or-die tension of Reigns, wrap it in a dating app — and you get a vampire deciding whether tonight's match is a partner, a friend, or dinner.

Why it holds

A dating app already trains the player in the input. Nobody needs a tutorial for a swipe. So the whole design budget goes into making that one gesture carry consequence: what you read in a bio, what you chose to believe, and what it costs you when you were wrong.

03 — The game

Profiles are puzzles

Love at First Bite is a dating sim built around narrative and investigation. You live the life of a vampire surviving through a dating app: swipe right on tasty humans, swipe left when a profile feels off, because some vampires are better at hiding than others. Matches are limited each day, and your thirst only grows while you decide on tonight's dinner.

The game started as our entry to the Indie Spain Jam 2023. The concept held up, so we took it through the PowerUp+ pre-incubation program and founded Honey Bun Games to build the full version, now heading to Steam.

Design highlights

  • Deduction as the core verb. Profiles are puzzles. Reading bios, photos and chat behaviour to unmask hidden vampires turns a swipe into a risk/reward decision.
  • A pressure economy. Limited daily matches and a growing thirst meter create a soft timer that forces commitment without hard fail states.
  • Dates as minigames. Conversations run on a tension bar: keep the date charmed, don't let suspicion peak.

The substructure

Every one of those is the same shape underneath: a resource the player spends without a visible number, and a read they make under time pressure. Thirst, matches and suspicion are three faces of one economy — which is why tuning one of them moves the whole game.
