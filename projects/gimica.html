Designing a Live Mobile F2P Portfolio

As the sole game designer for a large free-to-play mobile portfolio, I design, build and test the systems and levels behind many live games — end to end, from hypothesis to rollout.

The context

Gimica runs a large portfolio of free-to-play mobile games. I act as the portfolio's sole game designer, working across game design, development, product, UX and QA — often on the same feature. That breadth is the job: a booster idea isn't done until it's specced, built, tested, measured and live.

Systems that scale across games

The most leveraged work is reusable systems design. I've designed and shipped boosters, combo systems, rewarded-video minigames and chest-reward systems that are deployed across many titles in the portfolio — designed once, themed per game, tuned per audience.

Levels by the hundreds

I designed the adaptive difficulty and level-progression systems and have authored hundreds of levels. At portfolio scale, level design becomes a pipeline problem as much as a craft problem: building difficulty curves that keep players challenged but never stuck, and tooling that makes authoring fast without flattening variety.

From hypothesis to rollout

  • Plan and run A/B tests end to end — hypothesis, variant design, rollout, readout.
  • Own features from design through implementation, QA and launch.
  • Prioritize roadmaps and coordinate work across art, development and QA.
  • Started a recurring cross-functional design review for the team.

Technical design

I build what I design: shipped features in Unity, with additional work in Babylon.js and Godot, and engineered tools and game systems solo from prototype to release. Specs are written for hand-off — clear enough that engineering and art can build from them without me in the room.
